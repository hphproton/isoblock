## Scope

Two kinds of work that use this tool asked for the same thing in different words. Image generation by layers needs, per object, the shape it has on the screen and the part of it that is not covered, to cut objects from pictures, place them in drawing order and keep a layer of the objects that stand in front of walking areas. Layout work needs to know how much of each object shows in a frame region (an object hidden behind others can go) and how much of a region the objects cover. Both are answered by one raster of the objects in drawing order. SPEC section 17 listed the second as asked for and not scheduled; section 14 listed per-object masks as not scheduled.

## Decisions

- **One object raster** (SPEC 14.1). Each pixel takes the object of the last part outline that contains its center, in the painter's order of section 13.4, in a state. A part's outline is the convex hull of its 8 projected corners, the shape the faces that face the camera cover. Vertices are snapped to 1/256 px and the top-left rule of section 13.6 decides pixels on an edge, so the test is exact in integers: two runs and the editor give the same pixels, and two implementations differ only where the last bits of a projected coordinate land on either side of a 1/256 step. The fixtures carry tolerances for that.
- **Masks** (`export --target masks`). Full and visible masks per object and per tag group, cut to their boxes, and one 16-bit label image of the whole frame whose values are the objects' positions in the file, so they do not change between states. `--grow` adds a band in pixels (Chebyshev distance) for the detail art adds outside a block. Masks are PNG files written by `src/cli` with `node:zlib`; the core computes the raster. No new dependency.
- **`visibility`** reports, for a region (default the whole raster), the full and visible pixels of each object and each tag, their shares, the share of the region each covers, and how much of the region is covered. Regions use half-open pixel ranges, so regions that share an edge share no pixel.
- **`visible_share`** is the check form: listed objects (by `ids` or by `tag`) must show at least `min` of their own pixels in a region. Objects without a pixel there are not examined; none examined is `skip`. In the editor it waits for the drop, like the grid checks.
- **`occluders`** lists the objects that hide part of an actor somewhere in a lane or a zone, with how many positions and the largest overlap. It uses the painter's (geometric) order, not the engine's keys: with the engine rule, a flat mat in front of an actor's feet and a deck under an actor standing on it count as occluders because their keys are larger, although neither hides the actor. Where `sort_consistency` finds no mismatch both agree; SPEC 18 says so.
- **Not in this stage:** editor overlays of masks, shares and occluders (stage 14); masks of zones, lanes or regions; a height budget and pruning to a region (still asked for, not scheduled). No change to the scene format beyond the new check, none to the runtime file.
- **After stage 13** (`ROADMAP.md`): stage 14, the editor explains (relation panel, Solve as a ghost, overlays of sort points, occlusion rays, shares, masks and occluders, editing hidden parts and regions, a `warn` level); stage 15, lanes and levels, designed in general terms: a lane is a polyline in any direction with a height per point, every lane check and relation works on that shape with the results of 0.13.0 for two-point lanes along an axis, `reachable` crosses levels through the lanes that join them, and a relation `faces` with a solver that may turn objects.
