## Asked for

SPEC section 17 now lists four more requests, which are not scheduled. Each one came with a minimal case, what users do now instead, and what that costs:

- **Surfaces at their heights in depth order.** Zones and lanes are drawn under every object, whatever their height (sections 14 and 18). A minimal case at an elevation of 20 degrees has a channel zone at h -1, 2 wide, between two bank zones at h 0, and a box 3 x 1 x 0.6 resting at h -1 in the channel against the near bank. The box is drawn whole over the near bank, which should hide part of it. Users now lay flat cover objects along a sunken surface's near edge, draw a raised surface as objects, and build a ramp that must show as flat boxes in steps (a rise of 1 over 12 as 12 boxes). A lane can climb since 0.15.0, but it is still drawn under the objects. Asked for: zones and lanes drawn at their heights in depth order with the objects, and a surface whose height varies along it.
- **A screen clearance check.** `visible` passes when a thin pole stands in front of a group of objects, because the pole hides only a few percent of the group. Asked for: in a frame region, no object with a given tag drawn over the drawn outline of given objects, or within N px of it. The check counts objects in front, and objects behind whose drawing rises out of that outline, and reports each offending object with its distance in px. Users now compare screen boxes in a script.
- **`solve` along a line.** Asked for: place the members of a row (one type, on one line) again so that the checks pass. The solver keeps a target spacing between neighbours on each side of the stretches the row must leave free, such as crossing lanes or a clearance area. It leaves members far from those stretches where they are, and leaves out a member when no place keeps the spacing. Users now search a 0.25 grid per row in a script.
- **`no_overlap` on parts.** The footprint used by `no_overlap` is the `size` rectangle, even when parts stick out (section 6). So a part outside its object's footprint, such as an eave, a balcony, a crossarm or a tree crown, can pass into another object unseen. Checked with 0.15.0:
  - a box 3.2 x 2.4 x 6 has a part 2.8 x 0.28 x 0.15 at height 2.35 that reaches 0.28 past its front;
  - a post 0.2 x 0.2 x 5.6 has its back face 0.2 in front of that face;
  - `no_overlap` passes with "no overlapping footprints", and the post goes 0.08 into the part.

  Asked for: parts tested against parts, with the overlap per axis in the message. Users now test every pair of parts in a script.
