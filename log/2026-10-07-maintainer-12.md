## Design

- **Engine rule** (SPEC 13.4): sprites are drawn in ascending key, one key per sprite. The key of a footprint is `cu·(u0 + u1) + cv·(v0 + v1)`, rounded to 6 decimals. A first draft used the center, `cu·(u0 + u1)/2 + ...`; slice boundaries rounded to 6 decimals then put many keys exactly half a rounding unit apart (0.3166665), where implementations may round differently. The doubled form has no such ties.
- **Square sprites:** when `cu = cv`, one key per sprite gives the geometric order next to every actor position only for a square footprint. For a footprint w × d with w < d, an actor that touches the long side near a corner sorts wrong over a strip (d − w)/2 wide, wherever the key point is. Slices are therefore as square as possible: n minimizes |L/n − S|, at most 64.
- **Measure:** `sort_consistency` rates a mismatch by the area drawn in the wrong order (the intersection of the two projected outlines), not by a count or a key difference. Counting pairs flagged a roof that never covers the actor (the bounding rectangles overlap, the outlines do not). A tolerance on the key difference does not measure the error either: a tie can hide a strip 6 px wide.
- **Single objects** (actor 0.4 × 0.4 × 1.7, step 0.1, reach 1, 60 px per unit): walls 0.3 × 4 in 13 slices, a bench in 3 slices, square objects and a tree with a canopy: 0 px². A counter with an overhanging top in 4 slices: 19 to 47 px², depending on how the grid falls. A 1.4 × 1 box in 2 slices: 327. A 1.2 × 0.8 table in 2 slices: 234. A 2 × 1.5 shed (one sprite): 1,122 at that position, 546 in the fixture scene. A kiosk whose roof hangs 0.5 over the walkway: 409.
