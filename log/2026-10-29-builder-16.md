## Pull request

Title: Stage 15: lanes and levels

Description:

Stage 15 measures lanes that turn, climb, and cross levels, and it measures facing.

A lane is a polyline. Each segment longer than zero is a piece, widened by half the width and not past its ends, with a surface from one end height to the other. `lane_clear`, `lane_reaches`, `lane_apart`, `against`, `clear_of` and `occluders --lane` use that shape. A lane of two points along u or v keeps the results of earlier releases. The lanes those releases skipped are measured now: `lane` (`c6`), `edges` (`c10`), `measures` (`diag_shape`), `levels` (`steps_clear`), `relations` (`r17`) and the check changes of height patch 43. At a bend the outer corner is not part of the lane. On a lane that climbs, `occluders` stands the actor on the surface and reports `level` null.

`reachable` takes `toLevel`. The path may cross levels through lanes that join them. Without `toLevel` it stays on one level. `area` together with `toLevel` is a schema error.

`facing` measures the angle from a `front` (`+u`, `-u`, `+v`, `-v`) on a type or an anchor. `a` may be an anchor. `angle` defaults to 45 degrees, and `relate` takes `angle`. The violation is the excess angle in radians. In the editor the Relations overlay draws that relation as a short arrow with a head at the tip.

`solve --rotate` may turn movable objects that are not locked, in quarter turns. A turned footprint is under the view, overlap and grid rules, measured against the input scene, even when the search does not move it again. Two footprints may overlap only when they overlapped in the input, and a coordinate the turn changes is a multiple of 0.05. The conflict set is found with that solver, so a relation a turn satisfies is not named. Fewer turns win ties. The report adds `turned`, and the patch writes `rot` before that object's `move`, with offsets from the position `rot` gives. In the editor, Turn objects adds the flag, a turned object is drawn as a ghost, and Occluders of lists every lane with a piece.

`hide` and `show` find a part the way an anchor is found, so an id that contains `/` can name its part.
