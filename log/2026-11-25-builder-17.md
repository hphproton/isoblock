Title: Stage 16: draw order across heights

Description:

Stage 16 draws objects at different heights in a precomputed order, and places a moving actor by its box.

A part marked `"sprite": "own"` is sliced from its own box, after the sprites of the unmarked parts. An awning or a parapet can be sorted by that box. Both the key rule and the order rule use these sprites. On a scene with no such mark, `engine: "key"` matches release 0.15.0.

The static order is the order the pieces need. Ties and cycles break toward the smallest key, then the earliest sprite. Hiding an object or a part removes sprites or pieces and keeps the numbers of the rest. An actor's slot is the largest order among the sprites that must be drawn before it, or -1 when none must. The order rule draws scene sprites in ascending order and puts the actor just after its slot.

`sort_consistency` takes `engine`. `"key"` (the default) is one key per sprite. `"order"` is the static order and the slot, and the result then has `pairs`: the object pairs whose static wrong area is over `maxPixels`. Mark the overhanging part from that list. `no_overlap` takes `parts`. With `true` it compares drawn part boxes; without it, the size boxes.

`export --target runtime` writes `isoblock-runtime/4`. Every sprite has `order`. Keys and slices of a scene without an own part are those of `isoblock-runtime/3`. Export again, accept only `/4`, and copy the new Godot adapter. It draws by the order rule. `add_actor`, `move_actor` and `apply_state` place actors by their slot, and `actor_slot` reports it.

The object raster keeps each full mask for the box of that object's outlines. A frame of 12,000 by 8,000 with 300 objects opens in the editor, and its check panel matches `check --json`. When a check cannot be computed, the scene is still drawn, that row is `skip`, and a notice gives the reason.
